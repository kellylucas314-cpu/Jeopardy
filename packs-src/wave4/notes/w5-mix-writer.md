## Clues I was less than sure about (fact-checker, please look)
- n1 WHAT'S THAT THING? $400 duct tape: the WWII ammo-box origin (Vesta Stoudt, J&J's Permacel) is the
  standard story. I took out "silvery" because the wartime tape was army green.
- n1 PLAYTHINGS PAST $800 Silly Putty: Apollo 8's crew used it to hold tools in zero g. Crayola tells this
  story and it is widely repeated, but I have no primary source.
- n1 PLAYTHINGS PAST $400 Nerf: the first Nerf ball came out in 1969. Some sources say it reached stores in 1970.
- n1 FOOTY NOTES $600 offside: the clue simplifies the law. It leaves out "nearer the goal line than the ball"
  and the own-half exemption.
- n2 CHECKMATES $400 knight: "only piece able to leap over others" is the usual phrasing. Purists note
  that the rook passes over the king when castling.
- n2 TORCHBEARING TOWNS $2000 Cortina d'Ampezzo: this relies on the Milan Cortina Winter Games having been
  held in February 2026.
- n2 SELF-MADE $800 Heinz: "more than 60 products when he picked '57'" follows the company's own story.
- n2 DIAMOND MINDS $2000 walk-off: Dennis Eckersley is widely credited with coining it ("walk-off piece," 1988).
- e1 BALLPARK MEMORIES $800 Yogi: a friend said Berra looked like a Hindu yogi when he sat cross-legged.
  That is the standard account of the nickname.
- e1 HANG IT ON THE WALL $600 American Gothic: Grant Wood said the woman is the farmer's daughter, not his
  wife. The clue says daughter.
- e2 PAINT BY NAMES $400 Michelangelo: the Pietà is the only work he ever signed. This is well documented.
- e2 POSTCARD PICKS $1200 Mount Rushmore: Borglum died in March 1941, and the carving stopped in October 1941.
- e2 POSTCARD PICKS $2000 Liberty Bell: the name came from 1830s abolitionists.
