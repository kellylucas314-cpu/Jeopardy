## Clues worth a second look from the fact-checker
- n1 LOWER 48 LORE $600 (Florida): the Key West buoy says "Southernmost Point Continental U.S.A."; the clue quotes the marker's claim. It doesn't state that the buoy is the true southern tip, which it isn't.
- n1 OFF THE OLD MAP $600 (Jacques Cartier): he recorded "kanata" (village) in his 1535 account. "Wrote down the Iroquoian word 'Canada'" is the usual phrasing.
- n1 HOIST THE COLORS $200 (Canada): the stylized maple leaf has 11 points.
- n1 HELLENIC HIGHLIGHTS $1000 (Pericles): the helmet and long head come from Plutarch, so the clue hedges with "usually" and "perhaps".
- n1 BUCKET LIST STOPS $400 (Pisa): the 1990-2001 stabilization reduced the lean by about 45 cm (about 18 in).
- n2 HAIL, CAESARS $2000 (Diocletian): the cabbage remark is the traditional anecdote, reportedly his answer to Maximian.
- n2 THE OTHER PRESIDENTS $1200 (John Tyler): grandsons Lyon Jr. (d. 2020) and Harrison (d. 2025) make "were still alive in the 21st century" true, and it stays true.
- n2 SHE RULED $1600 (Cixi): the navy-funds story is widely told but disputed in its details, hence "is said to".
- n2 THIRST QUARTERS $400 (Sahara): "about a quarter is sand dunes" is the standard figure. Some sources say 20 to 25 percent.
- n2 THIRST QUARTERS $2000 (Rub' al Khali): Bertram Thomas, 1930-31, is generally credited with the first recorded Western crossing.
- n2 WATERFRONT CAPITALS $1600 (Montevideo): the "I see a mountain" origin is labeled a folk tale in the clue.
- e1 HOME, SWEET COLONY $200 (Mayflower): 102 passengers is the standard count.
- e1 TALL TALES & TRUE $200 (Paul Bunyan): the footprints-made-the-lakes story is folklore, and the clue says "Legend says".
- e2 LADIES OF THE HOUSE $1200 (Dolley Madison): kept general ("famous for serving ice cream at her parties") because sources give different years for the inaugural-ball ice cream.
- e2 ROLLIN' ON THE RIVER $2000 (Missouri): it's the longest U.S. river by the usual USGS figures (2,341 vs 2,340 mi), a very thin margin.
- e2 NICKNAME THAT CITY $400 (Chicago): the clue offers both origin stories rather than picking one.
- nf NAME CHANGE (St. Petersburg): its names were St. Petersburg, then Petrograd (1914), then Leningrad (1924), then St. Petersburg again (1991). It was capital from about 1712 to 1918.
- nf NAMED FOR DAD (Pennsylvania): the 1681 charter settled a debt the Crown owed Admiral Sir William Penn (d. 1670).
