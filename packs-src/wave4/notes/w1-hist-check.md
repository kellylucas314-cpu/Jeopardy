## Changes (before → after, reason)

- n1 CAPITALS BY DESIGN $800 (Islamabad): "this planned capital replaced Karachi as Pakistan's seat in the 1960s" → "this planned city was built in the 1960s as Pakistan's new capital". Rawalpindi, not Islamabad, took over from Karachi in 1959 as the interim seat; Islamabad became the working capital in 1967.
- n1 LOWER 48 LORE $600 (Florida): "A giant striped buoy in Key West marks the 'Southernmost Point' of the lower 48" → "A buoy-shaped Key West marker claims to mark the lower 48's 'Southernmost Point' in this state". It is a concrete marker painted like a buoy, and it is not the true southernmost point (Ballast Key and a spot on Navy land are farther south), so the clue now reports the claim.
- n2 HAIL, CAESARS $800 (Hadrian): "73-mile stone wall" → "73-mile wall". The western stretch was first built of turf and only later rebuilt in stone.
- n2 HAIL, CAESARS $2000 (Diocletian): "Begged to return to power, this retired emperor said he'd rather tend the cabbages" → "Urged to return to power, this retired emperor reportedly preferred tending cabbages". The remark comes from one late source (Epitome de Caesaribus), so it is now hedged.
- n2 CROWNS & CHRONICLES $1200 (Bayeux Tapestry): "230-foot" → "224-foot". It measures 68.38 m (224 ft); 230 ft is a rounding of "about 70 m".
- n2 CROWNS & CHRONICLES $1600 (Eleanor of Aquitaine): "Long before she was England's queen" → "Before she was England's queen". The crusade was 1147–49 and she became Henry II's queen in 1154, only five years later.
- n2 THE OTHER PRESIDENTS $2000 (Hayes): "His wife's ban on liquor at White House functions" → "A liquor ban at White House functions under this president later earned his wife the nickname". Historians (White House Historical Association) credit the decision to Hayes himself; Lucy got the nickname.
- n2 THE MANDATE OF HEAVEN $1200: queue / Qing → "Nine in ten Chinese people share an ethnic name with this dynasty, which ruled for four centuries" / Han. COLLISION FIX: the Qing-and-queue fact restated n1-w2-lit "Q, HOLD THE U". Han Chinese are about 91% of China's people; the Han ruled 206 B.C. to A.D. 220 (about 426 years with the Xin interlude). Han appears nowhere else in the w1-hist files.
- e1 HOME, SWEET COLONY $1000 (Roanoke): "vanished ... in the 1580s" → "Settled in 1587, the 'Lost Colony' vanished from this island". The colony was found gone in 1590, so the vanishing may not fall in the 1580s.
- e2 WORDS FOR THE AGES $1200 (Nathan Hale): "Hanged by the British in 1776, this young spy regretted having just one life to give" → "Hanged as a spy in 1776, he reportedly regretted having but one life to lose". The line is known only from a later report (via William Hull), and the remembered wording is "lose", not "give".
- e2 ROLLIN' ON THE RIVER $2000 (Missouri): "Nicknamed 'Big Muddy,' this is the longest river in the United States" → "Lewis and Clark's Corps of Discovery paddled up this 'Big Muddy' river in 1804". The longest-river claim rests on a one-mile USGS margin (2,341 vs 2,340 mi) and other measurements reverse it; the Lewis and Clark fact is certain and still easy.
- ef FROM FRANCE, WITH LOVE (Statue of Liberty): "bolted back together" → "riveted back together". The copper skin was joined with rivets.
- ef LET FREEDOM RING (Liberty Bell): "Abolitionists, not the Founding Fathers, gave this cracked Philadelphia landmark the name we know it by" → "Shipped from a London foundry in 1752, this Philadelphia icon cracked on its very first test ring and had to be recast". COLLISION FIX: the abolitionists-named-it fact restated e2-w5-mix "POSTCARD PICKS". New fact: cast at Whitechapel, it arrived in 1752, cracked when first rung to test it, and was recast by Pass and Stow in 1753.

## The writer's doubts, resolved

- Key West "Southernmost Point": reworded to report the marker's claim (above).

- Canada's maple leaf has 11 points. Kept.
- Pericles' helmet: Plutarch's explanation; "usually ... perhaps" is the right hedge. Kept.
- Pisa: about 45 cm (17.7 in) of lean removed in the 1990–2001 work; "about 18 inches" is right. Kept.
- Diocletian's cabbages: hedged with "reportedly" (above).
- Tyler's grandsons: Lyon Gardiner Tyler Jr. died in 2020 and Harrison Ruffin Tyler in 2025, so "were still alive in the 21st century" is true and stays true. Kept.
- Cixi and the navy funds: the diversion is broadly accepted, the amounts disputed; "is said to" covers it. Kept.
- Sahara "about a quarter" sand dunes: standard figure (20 to 25 percent). Kept.
- Bertram Thomas: crossed the Rub' al Khali December 1930 to February 1931, the first recorded Western crossing. "In 1931 ... became the first Westerner to cross" is fine. Kept.
- Montevideo "I see a mountain": the clue labels it a folk tale. Kept.
- Mayflower 102 passengers: the standard count. Kept.
- Paul Bunyan: framed as legend. Kept.
- Dolley Madison and ice cream: general wording is safe. Kept.
- Missouri as longest U.S. river: replaced (above).
- Chicago "Windy City": both origins offered. Kept.
- St. Petersburg: founded 1703, capital about 1712 to 1918 (a short Moscow interval under Peter II), Petrograd 1914, Leningrad 1924, St. Petersburg 1991. Kept.
- Pennsylvania: named by Charles II for Admiral Sir William Penn; the charter settled a £16,000 debt. Kept.

## Still slightly shaky (left as is)

- n1 BUCKET LIST STOPS $200 (Taj Mahal): the outward tilt of the minarets is real; that it was deliberate so they would fall away is the usual explanation, not documented intent. The clue says "so a quake would topple them away", which reads as intent. Low risk; consider "so that a quake would likely topple them away".
- n1 OFF THE OLD MAP $800 (Zheng He): the first giraffe (1414) came with Bengali envoys who sailed with Zheng He's fleet; later ones came from Malindi. "Brought home by this Ming admiral's treasure fleets" is the usual telling.
- n2 HIGH AND MIGHTY $2000 (Elbrus): 5,642 m vs Mont Blanc's 4,806 m is 836 m, just over half a mile. True but tight.
- nf A CAPITAL HONOR (Liberia): the settlement was renamed Monrovia in 1824, during Monroe's presidency (1817–25), so "in office at the time" holds.
