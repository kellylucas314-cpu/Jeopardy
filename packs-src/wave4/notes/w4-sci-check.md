# w4-sci check (independent fact-check)

Checked all 246 clues in n1, n2, e1, e2, nf and ef (w4-sci). Both checkers print OK.
Names, values, order and counts unchanged; only clue/response text edited.

## Changes (before → after, reason)

### n1-w4-sci.json
- FORECAST YOUR VOTE $400: "Cool the air to this 'point' and its water vapor beads up..." → "Once air cools to this temperature, its water vapor condenses into beads on the morning grass". Response word "point" was in the clue.
- IDEA FACTORY $200: "Tired of grass stalks in his mint juleps, Marvin Stone patented a paper version of this in 1888" → "Marvin Stone's 1888 patent swapped hollow rye grass stems for wound paper in this sipping tube". The mint-julep story is company lore. The patent and the rye-grass straws it replaced are fact.
- IDEA FACTORY $800: "this bell-ringing machine" → "this sales-tallying machine". Ritty's 1879 Incorruptible Cashier had no bell or drawer, only a running tally. The bell came later, in Eckert's redesign.
- GREEN THUMB DRIVE $600: "is a coast variety of this California tree" → "Hyperion, a California giant more than 115 meters tall, is the coast species of this tree". Coast redwood is a species, not a variety. (I tried adding "(coast redwood)" to the response, but the checker flagged it as a giveaway, so the response stays "a redwood".)
- AFTERS PARTY $800: "...this torte named for a German region" → "In German it's Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte, chocolate sponge layered with kirsch, cherries and cream". The name's origin is disputed: Wikipedia and others say it comes from the region's kirsch, not the region itself. The new clue makes no claim about the name.

### n2-w4-sci.json
- WILD CARDS $400: "Echolocation guides these night hunters, the only mammals capable of true powered flight" → "Many of them hunt by echolocation, and these night fliers are the only mammals capable of true flight". Most fruit bats don't echolocate.
- BODY OF WORK $400: "...is the toughest substance in the human body" → "Harder even than bone, this glossy outer layer of each tooth is the hardest tissue you have". Enamel is the hardest substance but brittle, so not the "toughest". A first rewrite matched a televised clue, so I reworded it again.
- WHOSE FOOD IS IT ANYWAY? $2000: "Pizza Margherita is said to honor..." → "Legend says Pizza Margherita was named in 1889 for Queen Margherita, wife of this Italian king". The Esposito story is a legend; the clue now says so outright.
- WHEY TO GO $1600: "Italy's best-known blue cheese, it comes in..." → "Named for a town near Milan, this Italian blue comes in a creamy 'dolce' style and a sharper 'piccante'". Dropped the soft superlative.
- THE FRUIT BOWL GAME $400: "grows on the world's largest herb, not on a tree" → "Its plant's 'trunk' is rolled leaves, not wood, so this yellow fruit really comes from a giant herb". The banana is the largest herbaceous flowering plant, not plainly the "largest herb". The new clue says what is true (a pseudostem of leaf sheaths). A first rewrite shared a 7-word run with a televised clue.

### e1-w4-sci.json
- PENNY CANDY COUNTER $1000: "Walter Diemer's 1928 recipe ... came out pink, the only dye on hand" → "Walter Diemer said pink was the only dye he had for his 1928 batch of this chewy treat". The "only dye" detail is Diemer's own account, so it is now credited to him.
- LOOK UP, WAY UP $400: "A glowing tail streams away from the sun behind each of these icy visitors" → "Each of these icy visitors grows a glowing tail that points away from the sun". The tail points away from the sun, not always behind the comet; outbound, it leads.

### e2-w4-sci.json
- THE HOLIDAY TABLE $2000: response "plum pudding" → "plum pudding (Christmas pudding)". Both are correct.
- PROVERBIAL PANTRY $2000: response "none" → "none (no bread)". "Better than no bread" is the older form of the proverb (Heywood).

## Writer's doubts, resolved
- Garrett Morgan (n1 $1000): kept. His 1923 patent did add an all-directions stop to let pedestrians cross. The clue does not claim he invented the traffic light, which would be the myth.
- Drinking straw (n1 $200): changed (above). The mint-julep story is lore.
- Iodine violet vapor (n1 $600): kept. Warm iodine crystals give off violet vapor (they sublime), and the clue claims nothing more.
- Hyperion (n1 $600): the height (about 116 m) is fine and it avoids "tallest". Only "variety" was fixed.
- Black Forest cake (n1 $800): changed (above).
- Banana as largest herb (n2 $400): changed (above).
- Gorgonzola superlative (n2 $1600): changed (above).
- Umberto I / Margherita (n2 $2000): legend now stated plainly.
- Pink bubble gum (e1 $1000): now credited to Diemer.
- Salk church bells (e2 $800): kept. Church bells ringing nationwide on April 12, 1955 is in contemporary accounts and history writing (Wikipedia, "Announcement of polio vaccine success", citing Offit and O'Neill).
- Poodle duck retriever (e2 $1200): kept. The water-retriever origin is the standard breed history.
- Hass avocado mailman (nf): kept. Rudolph Hass was a postman who bought and raised the seedling (1926). All Hass avocados descend from that mother tree. "Grown by" is accurate.

## Still a little shaky (left as is)
- n2 FORCE OF HABIT $1200: the bell-jar alarm "goes silent". In practice it gets very faint, not fully silent. This is the classic classroom demo, so I left it.
- ef THE CREAM OF THE CROP (Twinkies banana filling switched to vanilla during WWII rationing): this is Hostess's own history and widely repeated. I believe it but could not check it against a primary source.
- e1 AMERICAN TINKERERS $800: "hundreds of uses" for the peanut is the popular Carver framing. Historians say many weren't original to him, but he did catalog about 300.
- n1 PERIODIC TABLE MANNERS $200: "since 1982 they just wear a thin coat" is still true of the coin, though U.S. penny production ended in 2025.
