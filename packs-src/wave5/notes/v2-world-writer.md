## Worth a second look from the fact-checker
- n1 WORDS WITH PASSPORTS / jungle: the "dry wasteland" sense belongs to the Sanskrit root (jangala), and the clue says so.
- n1 BANKS OF THE WORLD / Zambezi: Kariba is phrased "one of the largest man-made lakes" (it is usually called the largest by volume).
- n1 HARD HAT HISTORY / Borobudur: "buried under volcanic ash and jungle" is the standard account. Raffles heard of it in 1814 and sent H.C. Cornelius to clear it.
- n2 THE RUSSIAN SHELF / Dostoevsky: 'The Gambler' was dictated to Anna Snitkina in about 26 days in 1866, against the Stellovsky contract deadline.
- n2 MIDDLE KINGDOM MATTERS / Long March: "fewer than one in ten" is based on the usual figures for the First Front Army (about 86,000 set out, about 8,000 arrived).
- n2 EAST OF THE ELBE / Budapest: the M1 (1896) is usually called the oldest underground line on continental Europe.
- n2 PERSIAN RUGGED VERSE / Ferdowsi: "very few borrowed Arabic words" is the standard characterization of the Shahnameh.
- n2 WORD TRAVELERS / taboo: Cook recorded "tabu" in Tonga in 1777, and his journals brought it into English.
- e1 WORTH THE TRIP / Terracotta Army: "no two faces are alike" is the widely repeated claim.
- e1 WORTH THE TRIP / Iguazu: Eleanor Roosevelt's "Poor Niagara!" is attributed, so the clue says "reportedly".
- e2 BEFORE COLUMBUS CAME / Pizarro: the "distant cousin of Cortés" link is hedged as "said to be".
- e2 BEFORE COLUMBUS CAME / Cusco: the puma-shaped layout is hedged as legend.
- e2 THE POLISH-UP / Copernicus (Easy row 5): a fair but quieter hook (Toruń birthplace and the lunar crater).
- nf AN ILL WIND FOR KUBLAI / kamikaze: the typhoons of 1274 and 1281 are hedged with "said to have wrecked".
