## Accuracy and wording fixes

- n1 BEFORE THE REMOTE $1000: "Dr. Richard Kimble finally caught the one-armed man" → "Kimble's long hunt for the one-armed man ended in this series' record-setting 1967 finale". Kimble never "caught" him: Gerard shot him on the water tower. The first rewrite also hit a seven-word run from the archive.
- n1 BRAVO, BRAVISSIMO $400: Porgy and Bess: "'folk opera' ... gave the world 'Summertime'" → "Clara's lullaby 'Summertime' opens this Gershwin opera set on Charleston's Catfish Row". This drops the "folk opera" fact, which a deployed Gershwin clue already uses.
- e1 SMILE FOR THE CAMERA $1000: "Kodak named its simple box camera for these elfin sprites" → "Kodak's box camera shared its name with these elfin sprites of Palmer Cox". Where the name came from is disputed (Cox's Brownies, or the designer Frank Brownell), so the clue now claims only what's certain.
- e2 THE FAT LADY SINGS $800: Marian Anderson "first Black singer to star at the Met" → "first Black soloist at the Metropolitan Opera". Her 1955 role was Ulrica, a supporting part, so "star" overstated it.
- e2 PIANO LESSON FAVORITES $1200: "nicknamed for this unit of time" → "shares its nickname with this unit of time, but takes longer". "Minute" here means small, not sixty seconds.
- nf WHY IT'S IN PARIS: "so it went to a French king rather than to Italy" → "and it ended up with a French king". The old wording was awkward, and Francis I got it after Leonardo's death, probably through Salaì.
