## Changes (before → after, reason)

- n1 PLANET HOPPING $600: "A single day on this planet lasts about 243 Earth days, longer than its year" → "This planet takes about 243 Earth days to spin once on its axis, longer than its whole year". Reason: 243 days is Venus's rotation (sidereal day); its solar day is about 117 Earth days, which is shorter than its 225-day year.
- n1 SEA THE WORLD $600: "Columbus's crew feared running aground in this weedy, windless stretch of the Atlantic" → "Columbus sailed through the floating weed of this Atlantic sea, the only sea with no coastline". Reason: the "feared running aground" detail is not reliably in the log abstract, and "windless" is the becalmed-in-weed legend. Columbus did log the weed (Sept 1492); NOAA calls it the only sea with no coastline.
- n1 BARK TO BASICS $600: "...Kon-Tiki raft crossed the Pacific on logs..." → "...Kon-Tiki raft sailed from Peru to Polynesia on logs...". Reason: it sailed Peru to Raroia in the Tuamotus, not across the whole Pacific.
- n2 SUM BODY $800: "In 1637 he wrote that a book's margin..." → "Around 1637 he noted that a margin...". Reason: the date of Fermat's margin note is only approximate (c. 1637).
- e1 BUG'S EYE VIEW $800: "Count this insect's chirps for 14 seconds, add 40, and you'll get the temperature" → "An old rule says this insect's chirps in 14 seconds plus 40 is about the temperature". Reason: it's a folk approximation (Dolbear, snowy tree cricket), not an exact law.
- e1 MOONWALKERS $600: "Apollo 11's command module pilot, he later ran the Smithsonian's Air and Space Museum" → "While his Apollo 11 crewmates walked on the Moon, he circled it alone". Reason: category fit. Collins never walked on the Moon, so the clue now says so up front. Same response.
- e1 BORN TO SPARKLE $400: "Dorothy's slippers in the 1939 film were this red gem" → "Dorothy's 1939 movie slippers were named for this red gem". Reason: the slippers are sequins, not rubies.
- e2 SAFARI SNAPSHOTS $1200: "can sprint about 70 mph" → "can sprint more than 60 mph". Reason: the 70 mph figure is a popular overstatement; measured top speeds are about 61 to 65 mph.
- e2 SAFARI SNAPSHOTS $1600: giant panda (bamboo) → "Pumbaa in 'The Lion King' is one of these tusked wild African pigs" / a warthog. Reason: category fit. Pandas live in China and are not safari animals.
- e2 SAFARI SNAPSHOTS $2000: polar bear (black skin) → "Famous for their 'laugh', these African hunters live in clans led by females" / hyenas (spotted hyenas). Reason: category fit. Polar bears are Arctic animals. Spotted hyena clans are female-dominated, and the hyenas kill most of their own food.
- e2 JURASSIC LARK $1200: "whose brain was about walnut-sized" → "famous for its tiny brain". Reason: "walnut-sized" is a popular myth. Endocasts suggest something nearer a lime or a dog's brain (~80 g).
- e2 SKY WATCHERS $400: "the easiest star pattern to spot" → "an easy star pattern to spot". Reason: an unverifiable superlative.
- ef FLURRY OF FAME: "photographed thousands of these in the late 1800s" → "Starting in 1885, ... photographed thousands of these". Reason: Bentley began in 1885 and kept going until 1931, so most of his roughly 5,000 photos were taken after 1900.
