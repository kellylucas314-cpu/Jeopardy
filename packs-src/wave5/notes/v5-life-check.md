Accuracy reasons for the rows above that were not repeats:
- n1 NAMEPLATE SPECIAL 400: "launched in 1901" is shaky. The first Mercedes was delivered in December 1900 and the trademark was registered in 1902. It did dominate the racing at Nice Week in March 1901, so the clue now says "first raced".
- n1 PASSPORT PROTOCOL 200: namaste is just as standard in Nepal, so Nepal is now an accepted alternate.
- n1 THE WORD ON THE FIELD 200: "soccer" began as British (Oxford) slang, so calling it "this American word" was wrong. Americans just favor it.
- n2 SPICE ROUTES 1600: Tamiflu is made from shikimic acid extracted from star anise (fermentation now supplements it), so "key raw material" is more accurate than "main source for making".
- n2 ORDER-UP ORIGINS 400 (writer's doubt): who coined "Wendy" (her siblings or Melinda herself) varies by account. The clue now says only that it was her childhood nickname, which is documented.
- n2 SWEET NAMESAKES 1600 (writer's doubt): the 1884 Battenberg wedding story is unproven. No recipe appears before 1898. The new clue gives the solid fact that the name is the Battenberg family's (anglicized to Mountbatten in 1917).
- n2 SWEET NAMESAKES 2000 (writer's doubt): who created the lamington is disputed, but the name is accepted as Lord Lamington's (Governor of Queensland 1896–1901). The clue now says the cake shares his name, which is a documented fact, not a legend.
- n2 BALL'S IN YOUR COURT 1600: precise. 17 named pitchers were grandfathered in 1920.
- e1 AT THE LUNCH COUNTER 600 (writer's doubt): the earliest diners were horse-drawn lunch wagons, so "Early diners were built to look like" became "Many old diners".
- nf COACH CLASS (writer's doubt): Naismith's 55–60 record is the only losing one in KU men's basketball history. But "the only coach with a losing record" could change in future, which breaks the evergreen rule. The clue now uses a permanent fact: he was KU's first coach and had a losing record there. It also names the University of Kansas, not just "Kansas".
- nf RING LEADER (writer's doubt): what Coubertin claimed in 1913 was that the six colors together reproduce the colors of every nation. He did not say each flag uses at least one of them, so the clue was reworded. The primary response is now the flag, because the white field belongs to the flag, not the rings.
