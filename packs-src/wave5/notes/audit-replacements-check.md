# Check of the repeat-audit replacements

Independent check of the 168 replaced clues listed in `packs-src/wave4/notes/repeat-audit-fresh.md` (98, Fresh n1-/n2-/nf- drafts) and `packs-src/wave5/notes/repeat-audit-v4.md` (70, `*-v4-nature.json`). Each new clue was checked against CHECKER-BRIEF.md and QUESTION-QUALITY.md for accuracy, one correct answer, clue and response agreement, category fit, ladder, giveaways, length and banned phrases. I also grepped every new response across public/data/original, easy, campaign and both drafts folders, and the names used in the new clues against other clues' responses. That grep turned up four repeats the auditors missed (Volvo, Atahualpa, Merv Griffin, Kumbh Mela).

I changed 12 clues. The other 156 checked out as written. No e1-/e2-/ef- files in wave4/drafts were touched.

## Changes (file · category · value: before → after, then the reason)

- n1-w1-hist.json · EMPIRE BUILDING · 400: "Captured by Pizarro, Atahualpa, ruler of this Andean empire, offered a room full of gold as ransom" (the Inca) → "Its people called this Andean empire Tawantinsuyu, or 'the four parts together'" (the Inca). Repeat of a different kind: wave5 n2-v2-world already asks for Atahualpa from the same room-of-gold ransom fact, and this clue named him. New fact: the Inca name for their own empire.
- n1-w5-mix.json · WHAT'S IN A BRAND NAME? · 200: "Latin for 'I roll,' this Swedish carmaker's name was first registered for ball bearings" (Volvo) → "Dallas-born in 1927, this convenience chain was renamed in 1946 for its new, longer hours" (7-Eleven). The replacement repeated wave5 n1-v5-life ('Latin for I roll... ball-bearing trademark', Volvo). New brand origin: Southland Ice's Dallas stores renamed 7-Eleven in 1946 for their 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. hours (numbers kept out of the clue). 7-Eleven appears nowhere else.
- n2-w3-screen.json · PRIZE-TIME TELEVISION · 400: "Merv Griffin, who also created 'Jeopardy!', dreamed up this letter-guessing game show" (Wheel of Fortune) → "In the bonus round of this game show, contestants get R, S, T, L, N and E for free" (Wheel of Fortune). Restated an existing Fresh clue reversed ('This talk-show host... created both Jeopardy! and Wheel of Fortune', Merv Griffin). New fact: the bonus round's free R, S, T, L, N, E.
- n2-w5-mix.json · FAITH-BASED FACTS · 1200: "Millions gather for the Kumbh Mela at Prayagraj, where this holy river meets the Yamuna" (the Ganges) → "Hindus keep bottled water from this holy river at home, and drops of it are given to the dying" (the Ganges). Same scene as wave5 n2-v1-usa ('pilgrims bathe where rivers meet at Prayagraj', the Kumbh Mela), and it named that clue's response. New fact: Ganges water (gangajal) kept at home and given to the dying.
- n2-w5-mix.json · FAITH-BASED FACTS · 1600: "Its fire temples keep sacred flames burning, and its dead were once left on 'towers of silence'" (Zoroastrianism) → "Its fire temples keep sacred flames lit, and its dead were traditionally left on 'towers of silence'" (Zoroastrianism). 'Were once left' implies the practice ended; Mumbai's Parsis still use towers of silence. Now 'traditionally'; 'burning' to 'lit' to stay near length.
- n2-w5-mix.json · PLAYING YOUR CARDS RIGHT · 800: "Off screen, 'Doctor Zhivago' star Omar Sharif was a world-class player of this card game" (bridge) → "Off screen, Egyptian film star Omar Sharif was a world-class player of this card game" (bridge). Naming 'Doctor Zhivago' as Sharif's film hands over the answer to an existing Fresh clue whose response is Doctor Zhivago (Omar Sharif loves Julie Christie). Fact unchanged.
- e1-v4-nature.json · SANDY SHORES · 400: "Boardwalk souvenir shops sold these little critters living in painted shells" (a hermit crab) → "Boardwalk souvenir shops sold this little critter living in a painted shell" (a hermit crab). Clue said 'these little critters' but the response is singular 'a hermit crab'; clue made singular.
- e2-v4-nature.json · ON THE RISE · 800: "Every October, hundreds of these float over Albuquerque at a famous fiesta" (a hot-air balloon) → "Every October, hundreds of these float over Albuquerque at a famous fiesta" (hot-air balloons). Clue says 'hundreds of these'; response made plural.
- e2-v4-nature.json · ON THE RISE · 1200: "Soft, bland background tunes like Muzak are often nicknamed this kind of 'music'" (the elevator) → "Soft, bland background tunes like Muzak are often nicknamed this kind of 'music'" (the elevator (elevator music)). Players will naturally say 'elevator music'; added it as the alternate.
- e2-v4-nature.json · BODY OF SAYINGS · 400: "Nervous before a big speech? You may have these fluttering in your stomach" (butterflies) → "Nervous before a big speech? You may feel butterflies fluttering in this" (the stomach (your stomach)). 'Butterflies' is not a body part, which breaks the category's pattern (thumbs, upper lip, Achilles' heel, eye). - n1-v4-nature.json · HUMERUS FACTS · 200: "Hands and feet hold 106 of them, more than half of an adult's this-many bones" (206) → "Over half of an adult's bones, 106 of them, are in the hands and feet; the full count is this" (206). 'More than half of an adult's this-many bones' read awkwardly aloud. Same fact (106 of 206 in hands and feet), cleaner sentence.
