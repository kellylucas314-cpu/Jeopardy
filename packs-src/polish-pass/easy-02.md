# easy-02 grades note

70 categories, 350 clues. All rewritten to the Easy Breezy register (50-85 chars, one sentence, no semicolons/em dashes, >=2 non-"This" openings per category). Validator: 0 errors, 0 warnings.

## Factual fixes / risk removals
- THE GIRL GROUPS $400: dropped "first girl group to top the pop chart" for the Shirelles (the Chordettes' 1954 "Mr. Sandman" makes the superlative arguable); clue now uses two of their hits.
- LEGENDS OF THE DIAMOND $1000: removed "last big leaguer to bat over .400" (breakable record); replaced with .406 in 1941 plus Korean War jet service.
- SOUL SURVIVORS $400: tightened Ray Charles clue to the verifiable fact ("Georgia on My Mind" became the official state song in 1979).
- THAT LOOKS FAMILIAR $600: "Napoleon built" the Arc de Triomphe softened to "Napoleon ordered built" (commissioned 1806, finished 1836 after his death).
- COMPLETE THE QUOTE $600: FDR "fear itself" clue necessarily contained the response word; swapped in Nathan Hale's "one life to lose for my country".
- TUNES FROM THE BIG SCREEN $600: removed banned engine phrase "this song".
- THE HILLS ARE ALIVE $800: rephrased Matterhorn Bobsleds as a 1959 Disneyland ride (accurate) without over-specifying.

Buzz Aldrin, Rhoda/MTM, Mork & Mindy, Martha and the Vandellas, Andrews Sisters, Rice Krispies, Jolly Green Giant, Doughboy, Charlie the Tuna, chicken pot pie, Night Court, Count Basie, Tommy Dorsey, Cuban Missile Crisis, silver lining, fair-weather friend, chicken noodle/tomato soup, barbershop quartet, Hank Aaron ("Hammerin' Hank"), Ohio/Mississippi/Colorado/Missouri River (responses shortened to "the Ohio" etc. since clue says "this river").

## Judgment calls / unsure
- Several subjects recur across categories in this batch (Supremes/Temptations/Stevie Wonder, Glenn Miller/Goodman/Ellington/Basie, Sound of Music/Singin' in the Rain/My Fair Lady/West Side Story, Aaron/Williams/Mays/Berra/Paige, Rio Grande). I kept the responses but gave each a distinct angle so no two clues read alike.
- Hoover Dam clue keeps "Boulder Dam" as the hook; "dam" is a 3-letter word so the validator ignores it, but it is a partial hint.
- Satchel Paige "age 42" relies on his 1906 birth date, which was long disputed but is the accepted figure.
- "Houston, we've had a problem" was first said by Swigert; Lovell repeated it, so the Lovell attribution stands.
